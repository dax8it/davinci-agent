# Reference analysis and visual style

The user's reference and project brief determine the look. This filename does not mean a style has already been approved.

- Record measured duration, aspect ratio, rational fps, orientation, audio format and color tags where available. Distinguish the finished reference's specifications from those of the new camera files.
- Observe structure: opening, development, key moments and ending. Note shot lengths, transitions, text/graphics, framing, camera movement, sound and pacing. Separate sampled observations from a complete watch/listen.
- Adapt the reference's editorial intent to the new content. An interview prioritizes complete ideas; a tutorial prioritizes visible steps; an event may prioritize action and natural sound. Do not inherit a band's montage structure for unrelated material.
- Choose landscape, portrait, square or multiple deliveries from the brief. Check faces, hands, products, UI labels and title/caption safe areas. A fixed centered crop or corner-camera layout is not universally appropriate.
- Match exposure and white balance before applying a creative look within the actual color-management setup. Natural, warm, cool or stylized treatments depend on the reference. Retain intentional scene lighting and protect skin, highlights and shadow detail.
- Match graphics to approved assets and check exact text, names, readability and timing. Keep components editable where supported. Verify captions against actual speech.
- Scope sound treatment by role: dialogue, music, ambience, narration and effects. Camera changes need not interrupt continuous sound. Do not apply speech cleanup or dialogue pacing to singing or intentional musical space.
- Inspect transitions and animation in motion. Flashing lights and screen changes can confuse cut detection; automatic timestamps are candidates. Frame samples cannot establish exact pacing, motion quality or sound.

Use [performance-recipes.md](performance-recipes.md) only for a relevant music/venue brief. Its fast-cut and cooler-grade values are optional examples from a specific production, not general defaults.
